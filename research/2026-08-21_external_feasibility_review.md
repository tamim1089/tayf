# TAYF / THE ROOM: Feasibility Verdict for a Free-Space 360° Volumetric Telepresence Startup

## TL;DR
- **The physics is largely sound, but the single hardest component — a display-grade swept-focus element running at ~2,700 Hz over a ~1 m volume with a useful aperture — does not exist as a buyable part in 2026.** The closest device, the Mitutoyo TAGLENS-T1, runs at a fixed 70 kHz resonance but has only an ø11 mm effective aperture and ±1 diopter (2 D total) range, forcing nanosecond-strobed illumination and a tiny light budget. The concept is not falsified, but it is gated on relaxing/inventing the focus element and a custom HOE band — both non-trivial for a solo founder.
- **Buildable at wedge-demo scale for ~$4–15k; NOT buildable as the full 24-engine 360° pod by a solo third-year student without a team and several million dollars.** The realistic path is: do the ~$1k focus-rate kill test, then a one-wedge floating-head demo, then raise on that.
- **The hub/ticket business reframe (own both ends, sell sessions) is directionally right but is a second, capital-hungry company.** Location-based entertainment economics are brutal (The VOID failed; Sandbox VR venues cost ~$250k–$1.9M and need >60% utilization). Fund the R&D via UAE deep-tech programs (Hub71 offers up to AED 750,000; in5/DTEC give near-free licences and a permitted lab) and prove the felt "no vergence-accommodation conflict" differentiator first.

## Key Findings

**1. The focus element is make-or-break, and the honest answer is "not solved for displays."** The requirement (~24–32 planes × 90 Hz ≈ 2,700 Hz plane-switch, ideally paired with a 10–20 kHz binary DMD) is met on *rate* by several devices but fails on the *combination* of rate + aperture + range + wavefront + light throughput:
- **TAG / acousto-optic lenses (highest-rate option):** The Mitutoyo TAGLENS-T1 (Mitutoyo acquired TAG Optics in 2016) operates at a fixed **70 kHz resonance** with a ~14 µs focal cycle — vastly exceeding 2,700 Hz. But its **effective aperture is only ø11 mm**, optical power range only **±1 D (2 D total, min 0.7 D / 1.4 D)**, transmittance ≥90% (400–700 nm), and because focus varies *continuously and sinusoidally* (never settling), it **requires nanosecond-pulsed strobed illumination** synchronized to the sweep phase. Mitutoyo sells a dedicated Pulsed Light Source (max 75 kHz, 50–100 ns pulses, max ~30 lm average output). Crucially, **US Patent 11,668,952 "Volumetric display" explicitly builds a volumetric display using a TAG lens as the resonant liquid lens with LED/DMD timing synchronized per depth plane** — the architecture is real and patented. Verdict: clears 2,700 Hz easily, but the ø11 mm aperture, 2 D range, and strobe-limited light budget are display-hostile at room scale. Price is quote-only (no public figure).
- **Polarization-switched Pancharatnam-Berry (geometric-phase) lens stacks with FLC switches:** n binary switches → 2ⁿ discrete planes. FLC switches hit **50–150 µs** (Ma et al., *Opt. Express* 27, 10079, 2019: PB lens 150 µs, FLC switch 50 µs, ~87% diffraction efficiency); polymer-stabilized PB elements reach sub-millisecond at >90% efficiency (Li et al., *Opt. Express* 27, 22522, 2019). Meta's Half-Dome 3 used stacked LC lenses giving **64 discrete focal planes**. This is the most display-credible path: discrete-plane, no moving parts, high efficiency — but strongly chromatic (needs per-color correction), and no one sells display-ready PB-lens+FLC stacks as catalog components in 2026 (it is a fab/research item; note ImagineOptix was acquired by Meta, concentrating this IP).
- **Deformable mirrors: wrong tool, confirmed.** Boston Micromachines Multi-DM offers only ~**5.5–6 µm** stroke (≈12 µm wavefront correction); ALPAO up to ~5 µm inter-actuator. Full-volume defocus over a large aperture needs far more sag than these provide. DMs correct aberrations; they cannot sweep a 1 m depth volume, and they cost ~$25k–$100k+. Refuted as the primary focus element.
- **Electrically tunable liquid lenses (Optotune): too slow, too small.** Best case EL-3-10: 2/4 ms rise/settle, ø3 mm; EL-16-40-TC (world's largest at ø16 mm): 5/13–25 ms with 150/600 Hz resonances that must be *avoided*. Even sparse-optimization driving reaches only low-hundreds Hz clean. Verdict: fails 2,700 Hz by ~10×.
- **The escape hatch is the strongest engineering move:** distribute depth across the N engines (each engine fixed at a different focal distance) and/or use a fixed multi-plane PB-lens stack. This **eliminates the swept element entirely** — the single highest technical risk — at the cost of more engines / fixed quantized depths. Love et al. (*Opt. Express* 17:15716, 2009) is the canonical fast-switchable-lens volumetric display; multiplane literature (Matsuda/Lanman/Luebke focal-surface displays; Meta Butterscotch/Half-Dome; Watt et al. on plane spacing) confirms 4–6 planes is often "good enough" for accommodation. **Recommendation: design the wedge around fixed/quantized planes, not a swept element.**

**2. The HOE band must be invented, but the fabrication ecosystem exists.** Covestro **Bayfol HX** photopolymer is the de-facto material (self-developing, RGB, roll-to-roll, available in >1 m-wide web rolls per the Meta Materials/metaAIR aviation case). **Ceres Holographics** (Scotland) does roll-to-roll HOE mastering + contact-copy replication with Covestro; **Covestro itself co-developed a 360° holographic display with Sony**. Large-format masters can be tiled from SLM-written sub-holograms (Bruder/Fäcke/Rölle, *Practical Holography* 2017; US 11,714,382 on vHOE replication). Angular multiplexing of many functions in one film is a documented Bayfol strength. Verdict: a custom angularly-multiplexed band is **fabricable via a contract partner (Ceres/Covestro/academic holography lab)** but is a bespoke R&D program, not an off-the-shelf buy. For the *single wedge*, substitute an off-axis parabolic mirror or a simple Fresnel/relay lens — the wedge deliberately does not test the band at scale.

**3. Nobody is doing 360° walk-around free-space *human telepresence* — this is a genuine white space, for both technical and commercial reasons.**
- **Free-space/volumetric:** Light Field Lab (SolidLight; **~$85M raised total, including a $50M Series B led by NCSOFT in Feb 2023**; ~471 global patents / 391 active; deliveries from 2025, built-to-order video-wall pricing, 100M pixels/m²) — but it is a *panel/wall*, not 360° walk-around, and not telepresence. Voxon Photonics (swept-volume; **VX2 ~$6,800**, VX1 ~$9,800, VX2-XL largest — but small tabletop volume, ~500M voxels/s). BYU Smalley optical-trap display (*Nature* 2018; single-particle, tiny, lab-stage). Swave Photonics (holographic CMOS SLM, sub-300 nm pixel, CES 2025/2026 awards — near-eye, not room-scale). CREAL (light-field near-eye AR/FLCoS). Sony ELF-SR2 (27" glasses-free 3D, single-viewer, eye-tracked). None do 360° free-space human telepresence.
- **Telepresence:** Google Beam (ex-Project Starline) shipped as **HP Dimension at $24,999 MSRP** (65" 8K light-field, six bezel cameras, one-on-one only, native Zoom Rooms/Google Meet; Beam software license sold separately at undisclosed price) — a flat window, not walk-around. Proto (full-size box from ~$29,000, "Epic" ~$65k, Proto M2 tabletop), Holoconnects Holobox (~€29,500 life-size, ~€4,500 mini), ARHT Media (subscription $15k–$40k/yr; **stock collapsed to ~C$0.02, ~C$3M market cap** — cautionary). All are Pepper's-ghost or transparent-LCD 2.5D illusions, most requiring dark rooms.
- **Why the gap:** the aperture/étendue clipping constraint (ΣD ≥ 2πz) makes true 360° free-space extremely hardware-heavy, and the two-ended telepresence market is a cold-start problem that killed Cisco's room business and stranded ARHT.

**4. The avatar is a bigger lift than the founder may assume, but no longer Meta-only.** Meta Codec Avatars are now Gaussian-splatting-based, generatable from a **phone scan + ~1 hour of server GPU** (URAvatar, SIGGRAPH Asia 2024), and Meta's SqueezeMe runs three full-body avatars in real time on a Quest 3. Open academic stacks exist (GaussianAvatars, Animatable Gaussians, Avat3r, Vid2Avatar-Pro, Relightable Full-Body Gaussian Codec Avatars). Real-time drivable, non-uncanny photoreal avatars from 4–6 cameras are **at the frontier of a well-funded field** — reproducible by a small team using open source, but the "non-uncanny" quality bar and live driving latency remain hard. The parameter-stream thesis (2–10 Mb/s, render locally at the receiver) is well-supported by the telepresence literature and is the correct architecture that decouples Dubai↔London.

**5. The hub/ticket reframe is directionally right but is a separate, capital-hungry business.** Location-based immersive economics: Sandbox VR venues cost **~$250k–$1.9M** to open (FDD range $250,719–$1,875,464), need **>60% utilization** to protect margin, and even the category leader (~80+ venues, ~150k players/month, ~$300M lifetime sales) relies on franchising to offload capex; consultant Bob Cooney publicly questions single-unit economics. **The VOID failed** despite Disney/Sony partnerships; Dreamscape struggled. Hologram-appearance willingness-to-pay is real but thin (ARHT per-event, Proto rentals). Verdict: the "sell tickets not pods" instinct correctly dodges the two-ended market, but a hub network needs a minimum viable mesh (you cannot sell a call to a single endpoint) plus serious capex — this is v2, after the wedge proves the felt differentiator.

**6. UAE is a genuinely favorable base.** Deep-tech incentives: **Hub71 (Abu Dhabi) offers up to AED 750,000** (AED 250k in-kind + AED 250k cash-for-equity via ADGM SAFE + up to AED 250k post-program top-up for further equity; ≈ US$204k), plus subsidized housing/office and Nvidia/AWS/Microsoft/Google-for-Startups credits; **in5 (Dubai) licence ~AED 1,000/yr** + desks + up to $240k cloud credits, no equity; **DTEC / Dtec SANDBOX** (Dubai Silicon Oasis) ~AED 9,500 join fee and permits a workshop/lab; **DIFC Innovation Licence ~$1,500/yr**; **Dubai Future District Fund** for later equity. Golden Visa for specialized tech talent (AI/engineering) is available with no monetary investment on the talent route. Optics/DMD/IR-camera import is generally straightforward for a UAE entity, though high-end SLMs/lasers can carry EAR/dual-use flags — check ECCN per item. Eye safety: **LEDs escape IEC 60825 laser classification and fall under IEC 62471** (Risk Groups RG0–RG3, measured at 200 mm) — a strong argument to use LED, not laser, sources; certify via UL/TÜV-type labs.

## Details

### Focus element — component-by-component verdict table (core deliverable of Research Task 1)

The decisive engineering insight: **the swept element is only mandatory if a single engine must serve all depths.** Because the design already uses N≈24 engines, depth can be distributed (each engine fixed focus) or served by a small fixed PB-lens multiplane stack (4–6 planes, per Watt et al. / Matsuda focal-surface work). This converts the highest-risk item into a solved one. If continuous depth is later required, the TAG-lens volumetric-display patent (US 11,668,952) is the proven-but-narrow-aperture route.

### HOE / relay band
Bayfol HX (Covestro) + Ceres roll-to-roll replication is the fabrication spine. Documented capabilities: >1 m web width, RGB multiplexing, multiple optical functions per film, self-developing (no wet chemistry), 8+ year stability (INAF telescope case). Master recording via tiled SLM sub-holograms (Bruder/Fäcke/Rölle). Sub-mm geometric coherence across 24 engines is more realistically achieved by **software calibration** (per USC ICT's AR-marker geometric + photometric calibration of 72-projector arrays) than by mechanical precision. Alternatives — Fresnel arrays, off-axis parabola segments, lenticulars, retroreflective sheeting, metasurfaces — are each cheaper per unit, but none give the angularly-multiplexed converging-wavefront behavior a custom vHOE band provides; metasurfaces are promising but not large-area/affordable in 2026.

### Prior art / freedom to operate
USC ICT's *Rendering for an Interactive 360° Light Field Display* (Jones, McDowall, Yamada, Bolas, Debevec, SIGGRAPH 2007) is the canonical 360° architecture: spinning anisotropic mirror + high-speed DLP, ~5,000 img/s, 1.25° view separation, patented (US 2014/0327748, assigned to USC). Light Field Lab holds ~471 patents (mostly panel/waveguide, not aperture-band 360°). The **aperture-band + pupil-tracked + real-focus combination appears to have filing room**, but a formal FTO search is required before spending on patents. The TAG-lens-volumetric-display patent (US 11,668,952) and Meta/ImagineOptix PB-lens patents constrain the focus-element design space; Korevaar & Spivey (US 4,881,068) is expired and free.

### DMD engine cost (verified, cheap)
TI DLP is off-the-shelf and fast: **DLP7000 (0.7" XGA) hits 32,225 Hz binary** with the DLPC410 controller; DLP9500 (1080p) 23,148 Hz; LightCrafter-class EVMs 4–9.5 kHz; the newer DLP500YX pairing reaches 16,100 Hz. Dev kits (ViALUX, Digital Light Innovations, In-Vision, TI EVMs) range from ~$3k to $15k+. The DMD is *not* the bottleneck — it comfortably exceeds the plane-switch budget, which is exactly why pairing it with a discrete-plane PB stack is attractive.

### Business model / unit economics
Google Beam/HP Dimension at $24,999 sets the enterprise anchor for *flat* 3D telepresence; a walk-around free-space pod would be far more expensive to build, validating the founder's refusal to sell $250k pods into a two-ended market. Sandbox VR data (venue capex $250k–$1.9M; >60% utilization break-even; franchising to shed capex) is the realistic template and warning for any owned-hub network. ARHT Media's near-worthless equity is the cautionary tale for hologram-telepresence as a standalone hardware+service play.

## Recommendations (staged, with kill thresholds)

**Stage 0 — The ~$1,000 focus-rate kill test (do this first, ~1–2 weeks).** Buy/borrow one fast focus device + a photodiode + a fast camera + a function generator/oscilloscope. Parts: a used Optotune EL kit or a borrowed TAG lens (university microscopy lab), a ~$200 photodiode, a ~$300–500 used scope, a ~$30 laser diode/LED. Procedure: drive the element, strobe an LED, and measure whether you can place and hold a resolvable focus at ≥24 quantized depths at ≥90 Hz each, with acceptable spot size and light throughput. **Kill criterion:** if no accessible element delivers ≥24 usable planes at ≥2,700 Hz aggregate with a spot small enough for a life-size head AND enough light after strobing, abandon the swept-element architecture and commit to the fixed-multiplane / per-engine-depth escape hatch. This test buys information, not capital.

**Stage 1 — The one-wedge floating-head demo (~$4–15k, ~6–10 weeks).** One DLP7000/9500 EVM + one focus solution (ideally a fixed 4–6-plane PB stack or per-engine fixed focus) + one off-axis mirror or Fresnel relay (NOT a custom HOE) + one IR-tracked viewer. Target: a life-size floating head with correct parallax and real focus over a 15–20° arc, where a viewer can *look past the image and see it blur* — the felt, demoable differentiator vs. Beam and every headset. **Success threshold to continue:** naïve viewers spontaneously report depth/presence and the accommodation–vergence agreement is measurable. **Kill criterion:** if the image is dim, ghosted beyond the ~126:1 ceiling even with a 2 cd/m² dark surround, or the focus cue is not perceptible, stop.

**Stage 2 — Fund the band + multi-viewer (raise on the wedge).** Only after Stage 1: engage Ceres/Covestro or a university holography lab for a small multiplexed vHOE test tile; add a second engine + viewer handoff. Apply to **Hub71 (up to AED 750,000)**, in5/DTEC for a near-free lab licence and cloud credits, and MENA/global deep-tech VCs. Use the wedge video as the entire pitch. **Threshold:** a 2-engine, 2-viewer, ~40° arc served by the band relay before attempting the full ring.

**Stage 3 — Only if physics and money both clear: the pod, then the hub network.** The 24-engine pod is a team-and-millions endeavor; the hub/ticket network is a *second* company layered on proven hardware. Minimum viable network is ≥2 paired hubs (a corridor, e.g., Dubai–London or Dubai–embassy district); you cannot sell a call to one endpoint.

**Talent/outsourcing.** Do not hire optics FTEs early. Buy university lab time (Khalifa University, NYU Abu Dhabi, AUS, TII), contract the HOE to Ceres or an academic group, and use open-source Gaussian-avatar stacks. Reserve cash for the wedge and the band tile. Golden Visa (specialized tech talent) eases later recruiting to Dubai.

**IP.** File **provisional patents only after Stage-0/1 results** narrow the novel claims (aperture-band + pupil-tracked real-focus 360°); a US provisional is cheap (~$150 government micro-entity fee + optional attorney). Before then, keep specifics as trade secrets and run an FTO check against USC ICT (US 2014/0327748), Light Field Lab, Meta/ImagineOptix, and US 11,668,952.

### Capital required by stage
- Stage 0: <$1,000 (mostly borrowed instruments).
- Stage 1: ~$4,000–$15,000 (one DMD EVM dominates; the rest is surplus/loaned optics).
- Stage 2: ~$100k–$400k (vHOE test tile, second engine, small team/contractors) — target non-dilutive/soft money (Hub71, in5, grants).
- Stage 3: several $M for a pod; additional $M+ per hub for a network.

## Caveats
- **Pricing gaps flagged:** No public list price exists for the Mitutoyo TAGLENS-T1 (quote-only), for Light Field Lab SolidLight (built-to-order), or for the Google Beam software license (undisclosed). These are genuine unknowns, resolvable only by direct quote.
- **Light Field Lab funding figure conflict:** most 2023–2025 sources (Forbes, GamesBeat, Tracxn) report **~$85M total raised** (incl. the $50M Series B led by NCSOFT, Feb 2023); one secondary source (getcoai.com) cites $135M. The $85M figure is used here as the better-corroborated number; treat as approximate.
- **The "no vergence-accommodation conflict" claim is physically valid** (light genuinely converges to a real depth) but its *felt* magnitude at conversational distance (~2.5 m, where accommodation demand is small) is unproven for this specific architecture and must be demonstrated in Stage 1, not assumed.
- **Contrast/ghosting is a hard physical limit:** the additive-image transparency problem caps at ~126:1 only with a 2 cd/m² dark surround — bright venues are hostile, constraining the hub form factor to controlled-light booths.
- **Several competitor figures come from trade press or company-supplied data** (Sandbox VR utilization, ARHT/Proto pricing, Voxon prices) and should be treated as indicative, not audited.
- **Open items (search budget exhausted):** GSMA Foundry and MENA carrier programs (e&, du, STC edge-compute/5G-SA monetization), and detailed Dubai venue-permit workflows (Dubai Municipality / DET/Department of Economy and Tourism, fire/safety for an enclosed pod). Research these before Stage 3.
- **Overall verdict for this specific founder (solo, third-year CS, Dubai, minimal capital):** The full 360° pod is **not** buildable solo. The **wedge is**, cheaply, and it is the correct fundable milestone. The concept is not falsified by physics; it is gated by the focus element (mitigable via the per-engine/multiplane escape hatch) and the HOE band (outsourceable to Ceres/Covestro/academia). **Do the ~$1k test, build the wedge, raise on the felt differentiator, and treat the hub network as a later, separate bet.**
