### 5. US11474597B2 — claim construction and file history
- **CORRECTION to the brief** [VERIFIED, primary Google Patents + USPTO full text]: the patent is assigned to **Google LLC**, but it *originated at Raxium, Inc.* (inventors Pulli, Wetzstein, Spicer, Jones, Maila, Economou → Raxium 2021-07-08 → Google LLC recorded 2022-08-25, corrective 2023-03-23). **Priority 2019-11-01 (correct)**, filed 2020-11-02, granted 2022-10-18, **status active, anticipated expiration 2040-11-02.**
- **Claim 1 (verbatim, USPTO):** "A multiview autostereoscopic display… comprising: a display area comprising an array of angular pixels, where: each angular pixel is configured for emitting light that varies across a field of view of that angular pixel; and the array of angular pixels is configured for displaying different views in different viewing zones… at least one eye tracker configured for (i) detecting the presence of eyes… (ii) producing eye tracking information… and (iii) detecting locations of the detected eyes… by performing a hierarchical eye tracking process comprising determining a location of the viewer, determining a location of a head of the viewer based on the location of the viewer so determined, and determining locations of the viewer's eyes based upon the location of the head… and a processing system… rendering a specific view for each one of the eyes so detected…"

Strong literal non-infringement position.
- **Infringement-risk argument (their side):** under doctrine of equivalents, a steered projector "acting as one angularly-varying pixel" (the USC ICT framing) could be argued equivalent to an "angular pixel"; the hierarchical-tracking limitation is your best distinguishing feature.

A distinct InterDigital/PCMS family exists in multi-view telepresence — confirmed **US10701318B2 "System and method for augmented reality multi-view telepresence"** (PCMS Holdings → InterDigital VC Holdings, recorded 2023-03-07). The "eye-tracking-to-reduce-number-of-views" concept the user remembers is actually described in the Google/Raxium 597 specification itself. **Next step:** Espacenet assignee search "InterDigital"/"PCMS Holdings" + CPC H04N13/366 (viewer tracking) + H04N13/305 (autostereoscopic) to pin the exact number.

### 12. Biometric and likeness law — highest-stakes item
- **UAE PDPL (Decree-Law 45/2021)** [VERIFIED, primary decree, uaelegislation.gov.ae + privacyarabia PDF]: "Biometric Data" = "Personal Data resulting from Processing, using a specific technique, relating to the physical, physiological or behavioural characteristics of a Data Subject, which allows or confirms the unique identification… such as facial images or dactyloscopic data"; biometric data is Sensitive Personal Data. **Is a *rendered* 3D likeness biometric data?** [ANALYSIS — lawyer question] The *capture/enrolment* scan is clearly biometric data; whether the *rendered output* is itself biometric data turns on whether it "allows unique identification" — genuinely arguable. **Safe design assumption: treat both capture and derived model as sensitive personal data.** Requirements: consent unless a listed lawful basis (Arts. 4–6); DPO where large-scale sensitive processing (Art. 10); DPIA for high-risk/new tech (Art. 21); breach notification to the UAE Data Office; cross-border transfer to adequate jurisdictions or with safeguards. **Executive Regulations status is reported inconsistently** — some sources say "issued 2023," others "pending." **[UNVERIFIED — confirm with counsel; do not assume final].** The **UAE Data Office** (est. by Decree-Law 44/2021) is the federal regulator; **enforcement to date is limited with no major public cases** (DIFC/ADGM have separate, more active regimes).
- **GDPR Art. 9** [VERIFIED framework]: biometric data is special-category only when "processed for the purpose of uniquely identifying a natural person." **Both readings live:** (a) a live-driven telepresence avatar is arguably *not* processed to *identify* (the person is already known — representation, not recognition), so Art. 9 may not bite on the render; (b) but enrolment/face-reconstruction processes facial geometry and, if any matching occurs, Art. 9 applies. EDPB Guidelines 05/2022 (facial recognition) and 3/2019 (video devices) support the "purpose" test being decisive. No settled CJEU case law on live photoreal avatars — genuinely open.
- **Right of publicity / personality rights** [VERIFIED framework — needs per-jurisdiction counsel]: US = state right of publicity (Cal. Civ. Code §3344 + common law; NY; new synthetic-media/likeness statutes); EU/UK = personality/image rights via data protection + national law (Germany KUG, France droit à l'image); UAE = image/privacy via Penal Code + Cybercrime Law Art. 44. A valid licence needs named grantor, defined uses, duration, territory, sublicensing, revocation, and explicit consent to *create AND retain a trained model*. **On revocation after training** — the hard problem: draft the licence so revocation triggers **deletion of derived models, not just source scans** (this is a drafting requirement, not a settled legal answer).
- **Deepfake / synthetic-media law:**
  - **UAE Decree-Law 34/2021 (Countering Rumours and Cybercrimes)** [VERIFIED, primary]: criminalises fabricating/processing recordings, images or scenes with intent to defame or harm; Art. 44 (privacy — recording/sharing without consent: imprisonment + AED 150k–500k); Arts. 52/54 (fabricated content: AED 100k–1M). The UAE Cybersecurity Council issued a 2025 deepfake warning; ~10 arrests in March 2026 for AI deepfake video distribution [SECONDARY]. **Your enrolment pipeline produces the exact artefact this law criminalises when misused** — consent and provenance controls are legally load-bearing.
  - **EU AI Act (Reg. 2024/1689) Art. 50** [VERIFIED, primary EC + law-firm analyses]: transparency in force **2 August 2026**. A live-driven photoreal avatar of a real person is a "deepfake" → **deployer must disclose it is AI-generated/manipulated; provider must mark output machine-readably** (marking grace period to **2 December 2026** under the AI Omnibus, in force 27 July 2026). Per the European Commission "Quick Facts," fines up to **€15M or 3% of worldwide turnover** — but **Art. 99(6) applies the *lower* figure to SMEs/startups** (a €2M-turnover startup caps near €60,000, per Vestbee analysis), which materially de-risks the financial exposure for you specifically.
  - **Does the AI Act classify this system, and at what tier?** [ANALYSIS — the user's flagged hole, now resolved]: the system is **not automatically high-risk** under Annex III (it is not biometric identification or an Annex III use case), but it is squarely caught by **Art. 50 (limited-risk / transparency tier)** because it generates synthetic photoreal content of real people. **So: limited-risk transparency tier, not high-risk** — the marking + disclosure duties are mandatory before EU shipment and apply from Aug 2026. **This does change the roadmap:** you need watermarking/marking in the render pipeline and a disclosure UX. Adding any biometric *identification* or *emotion-recognition* function would risk jumping to high-risk or prohibited (Art. 5) — keep those out of scope.
  - **US state synthetic-media statutes** [SECONDARY]: a growing patchwork (California AI-likeness laws, Tennessee ELVIS Act, Texas, Washington), mostly election/nonconsensual-imagery + name-image-likeness. Get US counsel per target state if selling into the US.
- **The four architectural requirements — validated/extended:**
  1. **Revocable, logged consent** — VALIDATED and required (PDPL + GDPR + publicity licence). Extend: tamper-evident, per-session log.
  2. **Deletion of derived models, not just source scans** — VALIDATED; the single most important control. Make it contractual because the law does not yet clearly compel it.
  3. **Cryptographic binding of a session to a live consenting person** — VALIDATED and *strengthened*: it proves provenance/liveness under the anti-deepfake laws AND *is* your EU AI Act machine-readable marking mechanism. Extend: pair with **C2PA content-provenance signing** to satisfy Art. 50 marking.
  4. **Data-residency choice** — VALIDATED (PDPL cross-border + GDPR transfer rules). Extend: offer UAE-resident and EU-resident processing; **default to on-premise/in-room processing** to minimise transfer exposure.

## Details and interpretation
The cost stack ($42k BOM → $50.4k factory → $54.4k delivered → $180k list at ~70% GM) is internally consistent arithmetic, but only the $2,195 DMD board is a researched, primary-source price. At ~$2,195/board the DMD subsystem alone across 15–19 engines is ~$33k–$42k at qty-1 pricing before discount, which means the rest of the BOM (optics, LEDs, cameras, GPU, chassis) must be tightly controlled to hit $42k total — optimistic at qty 1, plausible at qty 100 only with real ViALUX/DLi volume quotes. The HOE band is the true wildcard: the moat and the single-source risk simultaneously, and no one has quoted it; Ceres' demonstrated capability (≤1.4 m wide, A2 master area, automotive focus) does not yet match a 6.6 m² angularly-multiplexed band.

On law, the two roadmap-changers are the EU AI Act (build C2PA marking + disclosure into the render pipeline before EU shipment; obligations live from Aug 2026, though SME fine caps sharply reduce the financial downside) and the UAE PDPL/cybercrime pairing (the enrolment pipeline is legally load-bearing — the same artefact is both a legitimate avatar and a criminalisable deepfake). The FTO reality is that the Google 597 patent is probably designable-around, but Light Field Lab's ~391 active patents are the real exposure and require a paid opinion.
